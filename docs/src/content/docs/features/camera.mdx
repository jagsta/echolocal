On the Echo Show 5, Show 5 (2nd gen), Show 8 and Show 8 (2nd gen). The Echo Dot has no camera.

## RTSP

Off by default. Turn it on under **Settings → Connected services → Camera stream**, or with the
**RTSP server** switch (`rtsp`) in Home Assistant.

```text
rtsp://192.168.1.50:8554/main
rtsp://192.168.1.50:8554/sub
```

`main` is full resolution. `sub`, a smaller stream for previews and motion detection, is on the
Echo Show 8 (2nd gen) only. Set resolutions, keyframe interval and quality under
**Settings → Camera → Streams**.

| Device | Main resolutions | Sub stream widths |
|---|---|---|
| Echo Show 5 (1st gen) | **1280×720**, 960×480, 864×480, 640×480 | No sub stream |
| Echo Show 5 (2nd gen) | 1280×960, **1280×720**, 864×480, 640×480 | No sub stream |
| Echo Show 8 (1st gen) | **1280×720**, 960×480, 864×480, 640×480 | No sub stream |
| Echo Show 8 (2nd gen) | 1920×1080, **1280×720**, 640×480 | 960, 768, **640**, 480, 320 px |

Defaults in bold. The sub stream keeps the main stream's shape and is never wider than it.

ONVIF discovery is supported.

## Password

Set a password under **Settings → Connected services → Camera stream**, or with **RTSP password**
(`rtsp_password`) in Home Assistant. The stream, ONVIF and the snapshot then all ask for it, with
the username `admin` unless you change it (`rtsp_username`). Leave the password empty to turn it off.

Give a recorder or Home Assistant's ONVIF integration the same username and password. A player that
takes a plain address needs them in it:

```text
rtsp://admin:password@192.168.1.50:8554/main
```

## Snapshot

Home Assistant gets a camera entity with still pictures.

## Picture

**Settings → Camera** has a live preview and pages for **Picture**, **Exposure**, **White Balance**
and **Streams**, and **Reset to defaults**. Every setting is listed on
[Settings on the screen](/use/settings/#camera) and is also in Home Assistant. Camera settings
aren't copied by config sync.

## Shutter

With the shutter closed, the stream, calls, the camera preview and Mirror show **Camera shutter is
closed** instead of the picture, snapshots fail, and a privacy mark shows on screen. Home
Assistant's **Camera covered** sensor (`camera_covered`) follows it.

Muting the microphones with the mute button also cuts the camera, the way Amazon designed it. The
stream, calls, the camera preview and Mirror show **Microphones are muted** instead of the picture,
and video comes back about a second after you unmute.

<Aside type="caution">
Without a [password](#password), anyone on your network can watch the stream while it's on.
</Aside>
