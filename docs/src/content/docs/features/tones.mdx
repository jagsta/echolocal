A custom tone is a sound of up to 30 seconds stored on the device. Once uploaded, it shows up
in every sound picker: the device chime, message chimes, timer sound, alarms, intercom rings, the
wake word tone and the mute sounds.

## With the EchoLocal integration

The [EchoLocal integration](/start/integration/)'s **Tones** tab is the easy way.

<Steps>

1. Open the **EchoLocal** dashboard and the **Tones** tab.

2. **Upload** a sound file. **Trim silence** and **Even out loudness** tidy it up first.

3. Pick the devices to send it to. It's converted and uploaded to each.

4. Choose it in any sound picker, on the device or in Home Assistant.

</Steps>

The tab also renames, replaces and deletes tones, and shows which devices have each one. The
actions `echolocal.assign_tone` and `echolocal.unassign_tone` do the same from automations.

## With the device's actions

The device has three actions:

| Action | Fields |
|---|---|
| `tone_write` | `key`, `name`, `page`, `pages`, `data` |
| `tone_rename` | `key`, `name` |
| `tone_remove` | `key` |

- **key**: lowercase letters, digits, `-` and `_`, up to 64 characters, starting with a letter or
  digit. Writing an existing key replaces that tone.
- **name**: what the pickers show.
- **data**: the sound as **raw 16-bit little-endian mono PCM at 48 kHz**, base64 encoded, sent in
  pages of 32 KB (before encoding). `page` counts from 0; `pages` is the total.

To make the raw sound from any file:

```sh
ffmpeg -i doorbell.mp3 -ac 1 -ar 48000 -f s16le doorbell.raw
```

Then split it into 32 KB pieces and call `tone_write` once per piece, with the same `key`, `name`
and `pages`, or let the integration do it.

<Aside>
Tones are kept per device. Upload to each one that should have it.
</Aside>
